Music Intelligence Report

SoundCloud’s 2026 data report began as a printed zine. I designed the website that took it online, in thirty days, without losing the photocopy attitude.

Context

A zine full of data, and a launch date.

Each year SoundCloud reads its own platform data to show where music is heading: scenes that are growing, genres that are mixing, how people listen. For 2026, a freelance designer made the report a printed zine: photocopy texture, loud type.

In December 2025 I was invited to design its website. It would live on SoundCloud’s company site and be built in Webflow, the platform the company already uses.

The starting point

Spreads from the printed zine, designed by someone else. This is what the website had to carry.

Challenge

Thirty days, and three groups to approve.

The launch date was close, and the site had to pass through the Music Intelligence team, the designer behind the zine and SoundCloud’s leadership.

Each version had to hold the same things together: SoundCloud’s new brand identity, its new design system and brand guide, and an interactive experience that stayed faithful to the zine. All of it on desktop and mobile, and buildable in Webflow.

Process

How the home and chapter pages changed.

The first version was grey and spare, with the chapters in a horizontal menu. The home then went through a grid of cards before landing on one block per chapter, each with its summary and a way in.

I explored a top bar with every chapter. The site shipped with a menu and a side index instead.

Chapter openings started editorial and minimal, with type only. Later each one gained an image, so every chapter opens with the same photocopy texture as the zine.

From the original Figma file.

Key decision

A home that maps the whole report.

A zine is read in order, one spread after another. On a website, people land on any page, scroll and skim. So the home page gives every chapter a short summary and a way in, and the reader sees the full report before choosing where to go.

A dot index on the side shows where you are. Each chapter page then gets the room it had in print, paced for scrolling, and ends by pointing to the next one.

Templates

Every page designed, some made into templates.

With so little time, I designed all the pages and turned the ones that shared a structure into templates. The developer could build faster, and each approval round had fewer moving parts.

Mobile

The same attitude on a small screen.

I also designed the mobile version. Each chapter stacks into a single column, and the type keeps its weight.

Handoff

From Figma to Webflow, with the motion intact.

I guided the developer who built the site in Webflow, through the animations, the spacing and the alignment, so what went live matched what was approved.

Outcome

Live on SoundCloud’s company site.

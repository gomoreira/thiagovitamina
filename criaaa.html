Context

The same site, asked for again and again.

It started as a portfolio for a photographer, Samuel, at mostra.se. Other artists asked for the same thing. Each site looked different, but underneath it was the same structure.

So I turned the service into a product. mostra.se was the first site to run on it, with its own domain, and it still does.

Challenge

Everything a person needs, without me in the middle.

When I built each site by hand, I chose the address, set up the DNS and sent the invoice. A product has to do all of that on its own.

An artist should be able to create an account, upload work, choose a layout, pay and connect a domain alone, and get an e-mail at every step that matters.

Process

First make it work. Then make it right.

I worked in layers. The flows came first, with plain screens. Then the design system, and only then the details of the interface.

  1. MarchFunction

    Accounts, onboarding, payments, e-mails, plans and the first site at its own domain.

  2. AprilSystem

    A design system in code, rebuilt twice, with a single family of thin icons.

  3. AprilDetail

    The whole editor aligned to the system. Menu alignment, font rendering, spacing.

  4. OctoberPilot

    Public launch and the pilot with FMU students.

The way in

From the live site, November 2026. The interface is in Portuguese, for artists in Brazil.

Key decision

Your name is your address.

The first thing you choose is not a password. It is the address of your site. The field checks it as you type and says right away if it is taken.

On the free plan that name becomes name.criaaa.com from the first minute. On Pro, the artist connects their own domain from the settings, and the editor shows the two records to add and checks when they are working.

Your own domain

The hardest part was making an address belong to someone.

Every artist lives at a different address, and each address had to find the right site, keep people signed in and send links that land in the right place.

  • New addresses did not resolve.One wildcard record now answers for every name.criaaa.com.
  • “www” was read as an artist.A list of reserved names keeps system words out of usernames.
  • Sign-in stayed behind.Each address keeps its own session, so a one-time link carries it from criaaa.com to the artist’s address.
  • E-mails pointed to the wrong place.Confirmation, password reset and Google sign-in now return to the artist’s own editor.

Paying alone

From the editor to Pro, with nobody in between.

The artist picks a plan in the editor, pays on Stripe’s own checkout and comes back to their site already on Pro. An e-mail confirms it, and another one if they cancel.

Beta testers went through sign-up and checkout without help.

Layouts

The structure is shared. The look is not.

Each gallery picks one of four layouts: Cascata, one image at a time; Panorama, a sideways filmstrip; Dividida, two columns; and Mosaico, rows of varied heights. Four more are on the way.

On top of that, the artist chooses the fonts, the cursor and transitions, light or dark, where the logo sits on a three by three grid, where the menu goes and how it runs, the spacing and the text alignment.

How it was built

Built with AI, checked by hand.

I built the interface, the editor and the back-end functions in Lovable, one small prompt at a time. Big prompts broke things that already worked.

For the parts where a mistake is expensive (database rules, sign-in, domains, payments) I worked with Claude. Before changing anything, we checked the real queries, logs and Stripe data.

The quiet failures got screens too. An e-mail that does not go out lands on a list in the admin, with a button to send it again.

React, TypeScript and Tailwind · Supabase · Stripe Checkout and webhooks · Resend · Vercel · Cloudflare DNS · Google sign-in
